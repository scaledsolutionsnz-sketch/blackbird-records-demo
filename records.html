How the bins work

Dig properly, not politely

New pressings live up front, sealed and priced as they arrive. Behind them, the second-hand bins. Every one of those records has been through the same routine before it reaches you: cleaned, played or spot checked, graded honestly, then priced and filed.

It took months of that work before the doors opened in 2024, and it has not stopped since. Stock turns over constantly, which is the point. Two visits a fortnight apart are two different shops.

If you are chasing something specific, tell us. We keep a running list of what people are after and a lot of it turns up eventually.

Around the shop

More than a rack of records

The room does a few jobs. Coffee in the morning, browsing all day, and a listening setup that means you are not gambling on a sleeve you like the look of.

01

The listening lounge

Settle into the listening lounge, take your time with a record, and hear a whole side before deciding what to take home.

02

Coffee first

We open at 8:30am on weekdays, which is before most people want to think about records. Get a coffee, sit down, work your way in.

03

Local shelf space

New Zealand and independent releases get their own space, not a token corner. Dunedin pressings, Bay of Plenty bands, CDs dropped in by the people who made them.

04

The CD wall

Plenty of albums never made it onto vinyl, or only did once at a price nobody wants to pay. That is what the CD wall is for.

05

New releases

Reissues and new pressings come in weekly. Our Instagram is the quickest way to see what has just landed before it walks out the door.

06

Events in the shop

The room gets used for more than selling records, including community nights and clothing swaps run with Envirohub Bay of Plenty. Watch our socials for dates.
